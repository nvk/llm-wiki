## Key Radiological & Clinical Findings
- **Radiography (Plain Film):** Essential baseline imaging for CONGENITALLY HYPOPLASTIC PULMONARY ARTERIES; evaluates anatomical contours, alignment, lucencies, and gross calcifications.
- **Computed Tomography (CT):** Gold-standard modality for multiplanar delineation, density characterization, and staging of CONGENITALLY HYPOPLASTIC PULMONARY ARTERIES.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** High soft-tissue resolution with exquisite contrast resolution for soft tissues, marrow space, and fluid kinetics.
- **Ultrasound / Doppler:** Real-time, non-ionizing dynamic assessment with color and spectral Doppler evaluation of vascular flow.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with CONGENITALLY HYPOPLASTIC PULMONARY ARTERIES.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.

Pulmonary Artery Hypertension
Pressure Measurements and Patterns
of Pulmonary Vasculature
Pulmonary hypertension exists when the pulmonary systolic
pressure is greater than 30 mm Hg and the mean pressure
exceeds 20 mm Hg. Pulmonary hypertension may occur when
there is increased resistance in any part of the pulmonary circula-
tion from the pulmonary artery to the left heart. The type of
pattern of the pulmonary vasculature on the chestfilm depends
on the location of the abnormal resistance, the chronicity, and the
severity.
If the heart is structurally intact, the earliest sign of pulmonary
artery hypertension is a convex main pulmonary artery segment.
Severe chronic pulmonary artery hypertension also dilates the
hilar branches but, unlike a left-to-right shunt, not the peripheral
arteries within the lungs (Figure 1-33). The gradient of small
vessels at the apex and large vessels at the base is preserved.
