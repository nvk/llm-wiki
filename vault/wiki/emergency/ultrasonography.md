# Ultrasonography

**Domain:** [[emergency/_index|Emergency Radiology]] ([Emergency Radiology](_index.md))  
**Source:** Emergency Radiology: The Requisites (Pages 99–100)  

## Summary & Core Concepts
United States and most other Western countries. Extension to the hepatic surface is very common.

## Key Radiological & Clinical Findings
- **Radiography (Plain Film):** Essential baseline imaging for Ultrasonography; evaluates anatomical contours, alignment, lucencies, and gross calcifications.
- **Computed Tomography (CT):** tions or hematomas seen on CT. during the portal venous phase of contrast enhancement.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** High soft-tissue resolution with exquisite contrast resolution for soft tissues, marrow space, and fluid kinetics.
- **Ultrasound / Doppler:** ity of ultrasound to detect acute hepatic injuries. Liver laceration detected by ultrasound.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with Ultrasonography.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.

CT is the dominant imaging modality in emergency rooms
in the United States and most other Western countries. Im­

well as in the proper characterization of most injuries, are

Once identified, it is important to document the type and
location of such injury. In addition, it is especially impor­

enhanced blood and the potential for injury to central
­hepatic vessels such as the hepatic veins and inferior vena

lacerations or hematomas (subcapsular or parenchymal).
While many radiologists rely exclusively on morphological
descriptors in their report, it is useful to understand the
liver injury scale developed by the American ­Association
for the Surgery of Trauma (AAST) (Table 3-1). This grading
scale takes into account features such as the size of subcap­

trauma surgeons about the extent of the injury than in the
