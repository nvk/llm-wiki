# Congenital Anomalies of the Lymphatics

**Domain:** [[cardio/_index|Cardiothoracic Radiology]] ([Cardiothoracic Radiology](_index.md))  
**Source:** Thoracic Radiology: The Requisites (Pages 90–92)  

## Summary & Core Concepts
Mosby, 2009, Philadelphia. Radiology of congenital abnormalities of the chest. WB Saunders, Philadelphia, pp.

## Key Radiological & Clinical Findings
- **Radiography (Plain Film):** as a fissure-like oblique line on the lateral radiograph. Radiographics 10, 651–674.
- **Computed Tomography (CT):** pulmonary artery with transpleural collateral vessels: CT and MR appearance. Computed tomography and magnetic resonance of the thorax.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** Computed tomography and magnetic resonance of the thorax.
- **Ultrasound / Doppler:** Real-time, non-ionizing dynamic assessment with color and spectral Doppler evaluation of vascular flow.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with Congenital Anomalies of the Lymphatics.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.

Congenital Anomalies of the Lymphatics
There are four major types of developmental lymphatic dis­
orders that involve the thorax: pulmonary lymphangiecta­
a congenital anomalous dilation of the pulmonary lymph
vessels; localized lymphangioma, a cystic lesion occurring
in the mediastinum; diffuse lymphangiomatosis, a prolifera­
tion of vascular spaces, mainly lymphatics, in which visceral
and skeletal involvement are common; and lymphangio­
leiomyomatosis, a diffuse infiltrative lung disease charac­
terized by haphazard proliferation of smooth muscle in the
lungs and dilation of lymphatic spaces. Lymphangioma is
discussed in Chapter 13, and lymphangioleiomyomatosis is
discussed in Chapter 7.
