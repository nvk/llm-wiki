# Toxic Encephalopathy

**Domain:** [[emergency/_index|Emergency Radiology]] ([Emergency Radiology](_index.md))  
**Source:** Emergency Radiology: The Requisites (Pages 51–52)  

## Summary & Core Concepts
Wernicke encephalopathy. Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis. This acute demyelinating process mimics acute stroke.

## Key Radiological & Clinical Findings
- **Radiography (Plain Film):** Essential baseline imaging for Toxic Encephalopathy; evaluates anatomical contours, alignment, lucencies, and gross calcifications.
- **Computed Tomography (CT):** Gold-standard modality for multiplanar delineation, density characterization, and staging of Toxic Encephalopathy.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** surrounding vasogenic edema (“T2 shine-through”). T1-weighted image shows minimal enhancement.
- **Ultrasound / Doppler:** Real-time, non-ionizing dynamic assessment with color and spectral Doppler evaluation of vascular flow.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with Toxic Encephalopathy.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.
