- **Computed Tomography (CT):** Gold-standard modality for multiplanar delineation, density characterization, and staging of Distribution of Disease within the Lung.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with Distribution of Disease within the Lung.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.

Distribution of Disease within the Lung
The distribution of disease in the lungs may be a clue to
the diagnosis. Gravity is often an important factor. For
example, aspiration pneumonia typically occurs in the
­posterior segments of the upper lobes and superior seg­
ments of the lower lobes when the patient is supine, and
when the patient is erect, the basal segments of the lower
lobes are involved.
Because there is much more bloodflow to the base of
the lungs than the apices in the erect position, certain pro­
cesses related to the pulmonary circulation occur at the
bases. For example, pulmonary infarction is much more
common in the lower lobes, and metastatic lesions tend to
occur at the bases.
