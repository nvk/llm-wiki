# Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome

**Domain:** [[emergency/_index|Emergency Radiology]] ([Emergency Radiology](_index.md))  
**Source:** Emergency Radiology: The Requisites (Pages 50–51)  

## Summary & Core Concepts
Multiple sclerosis—optic neuritis. A, FLAIR image shows a few white matter hyperintensities. C, Coronal STIR image shows asymmetric hyperintensity of the right optic nerve (arrow).

## Key Radiological & Clinical Findings
- **Radiography (Plain Film):** Essential baseline imaging for Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome; evaluates anatomical contours, alignment, lucencies, and gross calcifications.
- **Computed Tomography (CT):** Gold-standard modality for multiplanar delineation, density characterization, and staging of Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** tected on postgadolinium T1-weighted image. enhancement of the right optic nerve (arrow) is shown on postgadolinium, fat-suppressed T1-weighted image.
- **Ultrasound / Doppler:** Real-time, non-ionizing dynamic assessment with color and spectral Doppler evaluation of vascular flow.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.
