## Textbook Reference Text
To my loving husband, Michael, for his unconditional love, support, patience, and 
encouragement through the long process of writing this book. I cannot imagine  
a better husband and father. Thanks for always being there for me.
To my children, Brian, Jeffrey, and Andrew and my grandchildren,  
Malcolm and Charlotte, for the tremendous joy and pride you bring me.  
I love you more than words could ever express.
In memory of my late father, Julie, and to my mother, Sunny, for instilling the love 
of learning and family in me. Thank you for encouraging me to pursue my dreams 
and supporting me through my studies and beyond.
To the many radiology residents and fellows I have been privileged to  
work with at Duke. Teaching and learning from you inspires me and makes  
coming to work every day gratifying.
To my colleagues and mentors, past and present, who have supported my career in 
ultrasound, shared their wisdom with me, and helped make this book possible.
Barbara S. Hertzberg, MD, FACR
To my late father, Bill, and my mother, Joyce, and to my past, present,  
and future residents, fellows, mentors, and colleagues. Barbara’s words  
above express my sentiments as well.
To my daughter, Dana, who will soon be an outstanding physician and my son, 
B.I., who will soon be a great lawyer. I could not be prouder of you and I cannot 
wait to read future chapters of your life stories.
To my wife, Mary, who is a soul mate, a best friend, a great radiologist, and a 
wonderful consultant all wrapped into one person. She tolerated my many nights  
in front of the computer and my weekends at the ofﬁce with surprisingly few 
complaints. She encouraged me to maintain high standards when I was tempted to 
make compromises. When chapters were completed, she was my editor and 
proofreader in chief. However, potentially her greatest contribution was providing 
positive reinforcement when I needed it the most. Her approval was the most 
gratifying aspect of this process and helped to make the whole effort worthwhile.
William D. Middleton, MD, FACR
