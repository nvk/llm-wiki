# Arthropathies

**Domain:** [[emergency/_index|Emergency Radiology]] ([Emergency Radiology](_index.md))  
**Source:** Emergency Radiology: The Requisites (Pages 186–190)  

## Summary & Core Concepts
The joint space is preserved. A joint effusion is frequently present. MRfindings have also been developed.

## Key Radiological & Clinical Findings
- **Radiography (Plain Film):** is abnormal before radiography. Initially, conventional radiographs are normal.
- **Computed Tomography (CT):** Gold-standard modality for multiplanar delineation, density characterization, and staging of Arthropathies.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** lined by a low-signal intensity margin. Osteonecrosis of the femoral head, MRI spectrum.
- **Ultrasound / Doppler:** flow (­initial phase) and blood pool (early phase) activity.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with Arthropathies.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.

Osteonecrosis of the Femoral Head
Avascular necrosis can be defined as ischemic necrosis of
bone affecting the epyphyseal or subarticular locations.
Histopathologicalfindings are identical to bone infarcts,
but by convention the term “bone infarct” is reserved
for ischemic necrosis occurring in the metaphyseal or di­
aphyseal locations. Osteonecrosis, a more descriptive term,
can be used for any location. Potential causative factors
are multiple, and include trauma, medications (especially
steroids), alcoholism, pancreatitis, hemoglobinopathies,
radiation therapy, dialysis, hypercoagulable states, baro­
trauma, and storage diseases (such as Gaucher’s disease).
Often, however, a specific predisposing factor cannot be
identified (idiopathic avascular necrosis). Regardless of the
cause, the patterns of bone injury, reactive response, and
imagingfindings are very similar.
Even though there is no universally satisfactory therapy
for early stage disease, diagnosis of this entity before the
joint is affected leads to an improved long-term prognosis.
