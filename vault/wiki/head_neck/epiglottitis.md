# Epiglottitis

**Domain:** [[emergency/_index|Emergency Radiology]] ([Emergency Radiology](_index.md))  
**Source:** Emergency Radiology: The Requisites (Pages 65–69)  

## Summary & Core Concepts
B, Slightly inferiorly, afistulous tract between the collection and the inflammatory tissue is identified (arrow). CT imaging can be useful for further evaluation. T1 images of the brain.

## Key Radiological & Clinical Findings
- **Radiography (Plain Film):** Essential baseline imaging for Epiglottitis; evaluates anatomical contours, alignment, lucencies, and gross calcifications.
- **Computed Tomography (CT):** forward, CT imaging can be useful for further evaluation.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI):** lowed by multiplanar postcontrast T1 images of the brain. T1-weighted images, can provide excellent diagnostic detail.
- **Ultrasound / Doppler:** Real-time, non-ionizing dynamic assessment with color and spectral Doppler evaluation of vascular flow.

## Differential Diagnosis & High-Yield Pitfalls
- Diagnostic mimics sharing radiographic or cross-sectional morphology with Epiglottitis.
- Key discriminator: Contrast enhancement patterns, tissue composition (fat, calcium, blood products), and anatomical distribution.
- Clinical correlation: Integration with patient presentation, laboratory biomarkers, and acuity dictates definitive diagnostic pathway.
